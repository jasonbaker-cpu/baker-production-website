What to say

Learn the shape, say it your way. Never apologise for the gap — no "sorry I dropped off", no "that's on me". Lead with what you've got on, not with why you went quiet.

Before you dial

Stand up. Their listing open on screen. Target is dials, not wins — voicemail counts, a no counts.

Warmest name first, always. Someone who's booked you ten times isn't a cold call, and once you've talked to one human the next four are easy.

The nerves don't go before the call — they go about twenty seconds into it. More preparing is just more avoiding. Cap it: five dials, fifteen minutes, stop when the bar fills even if they all say no.

Same hour every day. 9–10am or 3–4pm. Never during a home open.

Past client — 2 weeks to a month quiet

"Hey [name], Jason from Baker Production — how've you been? … I was going through my calendar and realised I hadn't shot anything for you since [address]. How'd that one end up going?"

Then shut up and listen. That's the whole call. Don't fill the pause.

"I'm booking spring dates now — what've you got coming up? Even if it's not locked in I'll pencil you in."

A fortnight isn't a problem, it's a gap. Don't go digging for one — asking "was something wrong?" invents a complaint that wasn't there.

Long gap — 4 to 6 months

"Hey [name], Jason from Baker Production — shot [address] for you back in [month]. How've you been?"

"I'm getting my spring calendar sorted and you were on my list. What have you got coming up?"

No explaining where you've been. No "been flat out". It reads as an excuse and they never asked.

Name one real detail from that property — the pool, the view, the staircase. Proves it isn't a mass call.

"Send me your next one and I'll do the walkthrough video on me, so you can see how they're coming out now."

Book the follow-up before you hang up. A long gap closes on the second or third call, not the first.

Cold agent — free declutter hook

"Hi [name], Jason from Baker Production — real estate photographer here in Perth. I've gone and cleaned up two of your photos from [street] — pulled the bins and the benchtop clutter out. No charge, no catch. What's the best email and I'll send them across?"

One breath, ends in a question they want to say yes to. The email is the win of this call, not the booking.

"How'd they come out? … Good. Anything I shoot, that decluttering's included — it's not an add-on."

"What's coming to market in the next couple of weeks? Even if it's not locked in I'll pencil you in."

Don't tell them what's wrong with their listing — say what you did. The clutter is usually the vendor's fault and it's a sore point.

Send the images the same day or the call was wasted. Send them with Studio credits attached so there's a second touch that doesn't need another phone call.

Same agency as an existing client

"Hi [name], Jason from Baker Production — I do the photography for [colleague] in your office. Got a minute?"

"I've been shooting their listings for months so I already know your office's style — logo on the web images, your floorplan format, clean high-res, all of it. Nothing to set up."

"Have a look at what I did for [colleague] at [address] — that'll tell you more than I can. What's next in your pipeline?"

Never say the colleague recommended you unless they did. "I shoot for them" is true and does the same job.

When they push back

"We've already got a photographer."
"Course you do — everyone does. Not asking you to swap, just keep me for when they're booked out. Can I send my price list so I'm in your phone?"

"Not interested."
"No worries at all. Can I send the two cleaned-up photos anyway? They're already done." (Then leave it. Log it as Not now, not Remove.)

"Send me an email."
"Will do. Just so I send the right thing — mostly units or houses?" (Then actually send it, same day.)

"Nothing on at the moment."
"All good — that's why I'm calling early. When you do get one, what turnaround do you need? … I'll check back in a fortnight, alright?"

"How much?"
Flat, no apology, no hedging: "$299 Essential — photos, floorplan, decluttering included. Most go Premium at $375 with the drone. What sort of properties are you listing?"

"You're dearer than X."
"Probably am. What you get for it is the decluttering and the turnaround. Try me on one and judge it on the photos."

"Who's this? / What's this about?"
Don't re-pitch. "Jason, photographer in Perth — I cleaned up two of your [street] photos, want them?"

"Bad time."
"No worries — when's better, morning or arvo?" Then hang up. Don't push through it.

Awkward pause
Let it sit. Whoever speaks first usually loses.

Voicemail + follow-up text

"Hi [name], Jason from Baker Production — 0466 789 522. Did some work on a couple of your [street] photos, give me a buzz when you get a sec. 0466 789 522."

Curiosity, not a pitch. A message they have to think about doesn't get returned. Under 15 seconds, number twice, hang up.

Text: "Hi [name], Jason from Baker Production — just left you a voicemail. Cleaned up two of your [street] photos, no charge. Want me to send them through?"

The text is what actually gets replied to.

On the job — first shoot with a new agent

A first-timer becomes an account or a one-off, and it's decided on the day — not by the photos.

Arriving: "Just to confirm — [package], that right? … Anyone home? Anything I should avoid? … And what's the hero of this place, what's the thing that's going to sell it?"

That last question is the whole game. Better photos, and it says you're thinking about selling the house, not just taking pictures. Almost nobody asks it.

Messy room: "Want me to shoot this as is and strip the clutter out digitally after? It's included."

Leaving — three lines, this order:

"You'll have these by [specific time]."

"If anything's not right, reply to the delivery email — I'll fix it same day, no charge."

"Have a look, and if they're what you're after — what else is in the pipeline?"

The revisions line is what converts first-timers. Their fear isn't price, it's being stuck with photos they don't like and having to argue about it.

If they came in on an intro rate, say the real number on the day, while they're happy and you're standing there. Otherwise job two never gets quoted.

On the job — any agent, as you pack up

Best sales moment of your week. Full attention, they've just watched you work, nothing's gone wrong yet.

"What else have you got coming up? I'll pencil you in now."

"Pencil you in now" is the trick — it moves it from a thought to your calendar while you're standing there. Never "let me know if you need anything".

At the car, not in front of the vendor: "Anyone else in the office listing at the moment? I've got a bit of room before spring hits."

Say one specific thing about the house — the light in the back room, the ceiling height, how the block sits. Agents deal with tradies who grunt and leave. Being the one who noticed is most of why they call back.

If the vendor's there, say goodbye to them properly. They're a seller now and a buyer in six weeks, and they talk to their next agent about who took the photos.

After you deliver

24 hours after: "How'd the home open go? What's next — happy to hold you a slot this week."

Ask about the listing, not the photos. "Did you like them?" invites a critique; "how'd it go?" invites a conversation.

The moment they say the photos are good: "Glad they worked. Would you mind leaving us a Google review? Takes ten seconds, I'll text you the link."

Ask while they're happiest. That window is about an hour wide and then it's gone.

Studio — what's free and what isn't

Photos I shot → decluttering included. Costs nothing at the margin, makes the work better than whoever they used before, and it's a real reason to book you over a cheaper shooter.

Photos I didn't shoot → that's Studio, and it's paid. Their old listings, phone photos, rentals, stock that's been sitting there.

"Anything I shoot, decluttering's included. If you want to run your own photos or old listings through it, that's the Studio side — five bucks an image, same day."

Never give Studio away free on every shoot forever. Free-per-shoot trains them to never pay, and then there's no product. One trial of credits per account, then it's a price.

On every delivery email: "Want any of these staged or decluttered? Send the file numbers back and I'll turn them around today — $5 an image to declutter, $25 to stage."
