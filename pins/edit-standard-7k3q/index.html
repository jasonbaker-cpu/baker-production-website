The goal

Every Baker photo should look bright, clean and true to life: straight lines, clean white walls, a clear view out every window, and a soft pale-blue sky. The agent should never need to ask for a fix. If a photo looks grey, flat, cold or cluttered, it isn't finished.

The HDR merge is the main cause of flat, grey Baker photos. Blending the brackets gives clean whites and real blacks.

Skies and exteriors

  • Day skies: soft pale blue with a few light white clouds, fading to warm cream at the horizon. Replace any grey, white or blown sky.
  • Never deep blue. Dark blue skies make the house look dark.
  • Clean edges. No halo around rooflines, trees or aerials after a sky swap.
  • Hard sun stripes across alfresco floors: soften them so they don't distract.
  • Lawns and gardens: green and healthy, not neon.
  • The front hero shot is the agent's main photo. Spend the most time on it.

Twilight

  • House lights on and glowing warm. Sky can be blue, orange, soft pink or soft purple, like a real Perth sunset.
  • Never super dark or heavy purple, and never a colour that looks fake. If the sky is darker than the house, it's too dark.
  • Use skies from the Dropbox folder Twilight Sky / Twilight picks - Perth dusk, a different one on each job.
Premium jobs

Premium natural day look

Premium jobs are shot as ambient brackets with the house lights off. They should look like 13/38 Third Ave, Mount Lawley: calm, soft and natural, like a magazine.

Do

  • Bright overall. No part of the room should look dark or gloomy.
  • Whites are clean and neutral, slightly warm. Not blown.
  • Keep a little shadow under furniture and in the far corners, just enough to give depth. Never let them go dark.
  • Light can fall off gently from the windows, but the whole room still reads bright.
  • True blacks in tapware, frames and black fittings.
  • Windows bright and soft. A gentle view outside is enough.

Don't

  • Don't leave any corners or ceilings super dark.
  • Don't add punchy clarity or saturation.
  • Don't use an HDR merge.
  • Don't make the room look lit by flash.

Retouching: remove vs keep

Remove obvious mess. Keep anything that belongs to the home. When unsure, leave it in.

Always remove

  • Loose cables, cords and power boards
  • Oil and water stains on driveways and pavers
  • Rubbish, bins and hoses in shot
  • Photographer, tripod or camera in mirrors and glass
  • Small marks on walls and ceilings

Always keep

  • Furniture, rugs, lamps, decor
  • Curtains, blinds, mirrors, artwork
  • Light fittings, appliances, fixtures
  • Plants and outdoor furniture
  • Anything that looks intentionally placed

Room by room

  • Bathrooms: keep the real tile colour and a touch of warmth. Never grey or black-and-white. Check mirrors for the photographer.
  • Kitchens: benchtops clean white with stone veins visible. Appliances keep detail.
  • Living areas: windows pulled, floor reflections kept, no blown sun patches.
  • Bedrooms: white walls neutral, carpet its real colour, window pulled on every frame.
  • Sun patches on floors: bring highlights down until the texture shows.
  • Dark or glossy floors: keep the reflections. Don't crush to black or lift to grey.
  • Ceilings: keep some tone. Downlights can be white, not huge glowing blobs.

Drone and client rules

  • Always replace the drone sky, even when it looks fine. Horizon perfectly level.
  • Read the booking notes and the job's Dropbox folder before editing.
  • Twilight drone jobs: you do all the editing. Then drag the photos into the Baker pins editor with the original DJI file names. Step-by-step guide
  • One Residential (Drew Dwyer): drone photos go out clean, with no pins, lines or text.
  • Belle Property: drone skies deeper blue with puffy clouds, to match Belle's own listings.

Final check before you deliver

Look at the whole set side by side. Every answer must be yes.

  1. Are all verticals straight and horizons level?
  2. Do white walls look white in every photo, with none grey or blue?
  3. Can you see outside through every window?
  4. Are there no blown sun patches or glowing ceilings?
  5. Is every day sky soft pale blue, and every twilight sky bright and natural, not dark or fake purple?
  6. Have cables, stains and reflections been removed?
  7. Do all photos in the set match each other in colour and brightness?
  8. Have the booking notes and client rules been followed?
  9. Side by side with the Crib reference, does yours look as good?
