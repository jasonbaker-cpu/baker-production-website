Guide for Perth agents & sellers

When should you shoot the house?

The sky behind this headline is Perth’s real sky, right now. Scroll and watch one house live through a day — then plug in your listing and see exactly when its front looks best.

The short answer

Shoot the front when the sun is on it and behind the camera. East-facing: morning. West-facing: afternoon. North-facing: almost any time. South-facing: even light or twilight.

Everything else — interiors, pools, drone, the twilight hero shot — has its own best window. Perth is at 32° south, so the sun swings through the northern sky. That single fact decides most listing schedules, and it’s the one most often got backwards.

Before sunrise

Blue, flat and still asleep.

Pre-dawn light is soft but cold, and the house has none of the glow that makes twilight work — the lights inside look like someone forgot to turn them off. Neighbours’ cars are still in driveways and the street is grey.

Mid-morning

This house is backlit. The one over the road isn’t.

The sun is up in the east — behind this west-facing home. Its front sits in its own shadow and the camera looks straight into glare. Across the street, the east-facing fronts are lit head-on and look their best all day.

Morning is also the calmest air of the day: the best window for drone, before the afternoon sea breeze turns up.

Midday

The hardest light for a front — the best for a pool.

In a Perth summer the noon sun climbs past 80°, almost straight overhead. Walls go flat, roofs bleach, and the eaves throw a hard black band across the top of every window. But water loves it: a pool shot at midday reads deep and blue, and interiors are at their most even.

Mid-afternoon

Now it’s this house’s turn.

The sun has swung into the west, behind the camera. The front lights up, shadows fall back toward the house, and the render gets its texture back. Same house, same day — a completely different photo from the morning.

In the warmer months the sea breeze usually lands from early to mid afternoon — fine for photos, harder for a small drone.

Golden hour

Warm, long and flattering.

The last hour before sunset turns render and timber gold. On a west-facing home like this one it’s the most flattering light of the day — and it’s the warm-up for twilight, so this is when the shooter arrives, sets up the frame and starts turning lights on.

Twilight — 0–25 minutes after sunset

The hero shot. Every light on.

For about twenty minutes the sky and the house lights balance. The sky goes deep blue to amber, every window glows, and the photo stops portals mid-scroll. It works on any facing — which is why it’s the fix for a south-facing front that never sees the sun.

By shot type

Different shots, different clocks

A full listing shoot is usually booked around its most demanding shot. If the front only works in the afternoon, the whole shoot goes in the afternoon — interiors don’t mind.

Sun on the facade

Front exterior

The shot that decides the booking. Sun on the front, behind the camera, not directly overhead. Use the planner above — it’s the whole reason it exists.

Almost any time

Interiors

Lights on, blinds open, and the windows are balanced in the edit. The only time to avoid is when hard sun is pouring across the floor in hot stripes — usually low morning sun into east rooms or late sun into west ones.

10am – 2pm

Pools & water

High sun puts light into the water instead of bouncing glare off the surface, so it reads clear and blue. The one time midday is the right answer.

Morning

Drone

Before the afternoon sea breeze, the air is calmer and stills are sharper. Sun angle matters less from above — block shape and context read in most light — but wind decides whether it flies at all.

Sunset + 0–25 min

Twilight

A tight window that moves through the year. The shooter is on site before sunset and the hero frame lands just after. It’s a $75 add-on — or a digital day-to-dusk from $1.50 when the timing won’t work.

Golden hour, ideally

Listing video

Video carries the light of the whole shoot, so it’s planned around the best facade time and finished into golden hour where possible. Wind matters for any aerial opening shot.

Season changes everything

Summer and winter are two different cities

Perth sits at about 32° south. The sun’s path shifts a long way between June and December, and the facades it reaches shift with it.

December

  • Sun peaks above 80° — noon facades go flat
  • Rises south of east, sets south of west: south-facing fronts get early and late sun
  • Sunset past 7:20pm — twilight is an evening booking
  • Afternoon sea breeze — fly drone early

June

  • Noon sun only about 35° high — soft, low light all day
  • Sun stays in the north: south-facing fronts sit in shade from dawn to dusk
  • Sunset around 5:20pm — twilight fits after an afternoon shoot
  • Short days: book fronts earlier than you think

What if the weather doesn’t cooperate?

Timing gets the light right; it can’t book the weather. Overcast and light rain are usually fine — interiors are unaffected and exteriors get a sky replacement in the edit, so a grey morning still delivers a clean, bright front. Flat overcast is actually kind to south-facing homes. If it’s genuinely rough — heavy rain or storms — we reschedule at no extra cost.

Missed the twilight window? A digital day-to-dusk conversion turns a daytime front into an evening shot.

Best time to shoot — common questions

When the sun is on the front of the house and behind the photographer. In Perth that means mid-morning for an east-facing home, mid-afternoon for a west-facing home, and almost any time of day for a north-facing home. South-facing fronts get little or no direct sun for much of the year, so they are shot in even light or at twilight.
The shooter arrives before sunset and the hero frame is taken in the 20 to 30 minutes after it, when the sky still holds colour and the interior lights balance with it. Sunset in Perth is around 5:20pm in mid-winter and past 7:20pm in mid-summer, so the booking time moves through the year. The planner above shows the exact window for any date.
For exteriors it is the hardest light: in a Perth summer the sun is over 80 degrees high at noon, which flattens the facade and throws dark bands under the eaves. Midday is fine for interiors and is often the best time for pools, which look bluest with a high sun.
From roughly late March to late September the sun never reaches a south-facing front, so it sits in shade all day. Shoot it in bright, even light, or make the front a twilight shot where the sky and the house lights do the work. In summer a south-facing front catches early-morning and late-afternoon sun.
Morning, in most of the warmer months. The afternoon sea breeze usually arrives from early to mid afternoon and can push wind beyond what a small drone holds steady in. Calm mornings give sharper stills and smoother video. See drone rules in WA for the airspace side.
Overcast and light rain are usually fine. Interiors are unaffected, and exteriors get a sky replacement so they still look clean and bright. If conditions are genuinely rough, such as heavy rain or storms, we reschedule at no extra cost.

Sun positions are calculated in your browser for central Perth (31.95°S, 115.86°E) and are accurate to within a minute or two across the metro area. Trees, neighbouring buildings and two-storey walls can shade a facade the sun technically reaches — tell us at booking and we’ll plan around it.
